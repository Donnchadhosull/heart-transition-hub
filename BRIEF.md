# Design brief

This is the brief the current site was built to. Use it as the prompt when asking anyone,
person or AI, to extend or rebuild the site.

## Goal

Build a public information website about the move from pediatric to adult congenital heart
disease (ACHD) care. A clinician gives a patient a QR code or web address in clinic; the
patient reads it at home on a phone. Colleagues at other hospitals can share the same
address with their own patients. No account, no install, and no setup by any hospital.

It is done when a transition team and their patients, at any location, can use it as it
stands: all the relevant information is there, it looks like a professional hospital
website, and a patient who chooses to can email a self-check to themselves or their team.

## Who it is for

1. **Patients aged about 12 to 25, and their families.** Write at a 6th-grade reading level,
   speak to the patient directly, and never talk down to them.
2. **The care team.** Pediatric cardiologist, ACHD cardiologist, nurse or transition
   coordinator, social worker, pharmacist, psychologist, primary care clinician. Write for
   clinicians: concise, practical, referenced.

## What kind of site it is

- **Information first.** Every page can be read without answering a question. Do not open
  with a quiz, an age prompt, or a sign-up step.
- **A website, not an app.** A site header with menus, breadcrumbs, a footer with a site
  map, and ordinary pages that can be linked to and bookmarked.
- **Navigation by audience.** The header lets a visitor choose "Patients and families" or
  "Care team", and under "Care team" choose their own role to see what applies to them.
- **Tools are optional extras,** grouped under "My tools" and offered at the end of the
  relevant information page, never in front of it.

## Look and feel

Clinical and professional, in the style of an academic medical center website: white
background, deep navy and a single teal accent, clear type hierarchy, generous spacing,
simple line icons, cards for navigation. No playful illustration, no gamification, no
emoji. Works on a phone and on a desktop, meets WCAG 2.1 AA, and prints cleanly.

## Content

**Patients and families:** understanding transition and what to expect at each age; heart
conditions explained; living with a heart condition, including when to call the team and
when to call 911; mood and wellbeing; learning, attention and memory; insurance, college and
work; moving to adult care and finding an ACHD clinic; who is on the care team and what to
ask them.

**Care team:** for each role, their part in transition, what to do, what to cover with the
patient, and which patient pages to point to. Program resources: the six core elements, a
timeline, what a transfer summary should contain, and how to read a patient's summary.
A page with the QR code and a printable flyer.

**Optional tools:** a heart passport with a wallet card; a readiness self-check; mood and
worry check-ins; a learning and attention self-check; a summary the patient can download,
share, or email.

## Rules that do not bend

- **Privacy.** No server, no accounts, no analytics. What a patient types stays in their
  browser. The site sends nothing itself. Emailing is the patient's own choice, through
  their own email app, with a plain note that a patient portal is more private.
- **Safety.** Crisis resources (988, Crisis Text Line, 911) are one tap away on every page.
  Any positive answer to a self-harm question shows them before anything else. The site
  says plainly that nobody monitors it.
- **Honesty about instruments.** Use validated screeners word for word and name them. Say
  clearly when something is a self-check written for this site and not a validated
  instrument. A self-check of learning and attention is a list of concerns to discuss, not
  a neuropsychological assessment.
- **Sources.** Base content on the current ACC/AHA ACHD guideline, the AHA scientific
  statements on transition, neurodevelopmental and psychological outcomes, Got Transition's
  Six Core Elements, ACHA and CNOC patient materials. Link them. Do not invent citations,
  statistics or cut-offs. Summarize in your own words.
- **Clinical review.** Keep all wording in separate plain-text content files so clinicians
  can edit it. Keep a single list of everything that needs clinical sign-off, and show a
  visible "awaiting clinical review" notice until a named reviewer signs off.

## Deliverables

The website, publicly hosted; a QR code that opens it; a one-page printable flyer; a source
list with working links; and the clinical review list.
